For the GM. This page tells you who the monster is. Players should stop reading here.

The premise

The Priory of Saint Cordula sits on a shelf of rock above the Greave Pass, a day's climb from the village of Tarnwick, on the last consecrated boundary before the Unhallowed lands. Every dusk the brothers light a fire on the bell tower so Tarnwick knows they are alive. Three nights ago the fire did not burn.

What Tarnwick does not know: in autumn the brothers cut a figure out of the glacier above the priory. It was a man, or shaped like one, kneeling, perfect, with frost on its eyelashes. The Prior called it the Ice Saint and sent to Aubrecht for a canon to certify it. Pilgrims would come. The priory would be rich.

It was not a saint. The brothers laid it in the chapel to thaw. Old Brother Faustin sat vigil beside it through the night, alone. By morning the ice coffin was empty and Faustin said he had seen nothing.

It is called the Guest in this book, because that is what it does: it comes in, sits at your table and wears a face you know. It can become anyone it is left alone with for long enough. Every piece of it is all of it. Fire is the only thing that ends it.

The hook

The heroes are in Tarnwick when the fire fails for the third night. The reeve pays them a purse of clipped coin each, with a promise of more in spring, to carry the winter supply up to the priory and come back with news. They lead one mule: lamp oil, salt, flour and candles.

“If the fire burns on the tower when you get there, they've only run out of oil. Give them ours and come down. If it doesn't burn, come down faster.”Reeve Mathilde Sallow of Tarnwick

Make sure the players know two things before play: the pass closes in heavy snow, and nobody will come looking for them until spring.

The shape of the adventure

  1. The Last Lamp. Arrive in a storm, get inside, meet the survivors, find the empty coffin. The pass closes behind them.
  2. The Byre. The first night. The animals scream. Something is wearing Brother Odo, badly. Now everyone knows it copies people.
  3. The Cut. Climb to the dig on the glacier and learn what the Guest is, and the one test that tells it apart. Come back to find the gate barred against you.
  4. The Chapter House. Everyone is tied to the benches and tested, one at a time, while the fire burns down.
  5. The Icehouse. What is left of the Guest has gone below, among the winter dead, and is boring its way down the mountain towards Tarnwick. Burn it, or warn the valley, or both.

Who is the Guest at the start? Brother Faustin. He has been the Guest since the night of the vigil. In scene 2 a piece of him is taking Brother Odo in the byre. When the Horror first reaches 7 (or at the start of scene 4 if it has not), the Guest takes a second person: use the roster below. Keep both secret until the blood says otherwise.

What the Guest is and does

Nobody on the mountain knows where it came from. The ruin it was frozen in, Ossk Varel, is older than the Binding and has no name anyone can read. Do not explain it further. The less the players know, the better this works.

Its rules

  • It takes people in private. Anyone alone with it, out of sight, for about a quarter of an hour can be taken. Afterwards they look, speak and remember like themselves. They believe nothing is wrong only for as long as it suits the Guest.
  • Every piece is all of it. A severed hand crawls. A drop of its blood flinches. Cut it in half and you have two problems.
  • Steel hurts it. Only fire ends it. A Guest form brought to 0 HP by anything except fire is not dead. It collapses, and one Crawling Piece slips out of the remains and hides, unless a hero burns the remains before the end of the next round (an Action with an open flame: a torch, a lit lamp, lamp oil, Kindle).
  • It breaks apart when hurt. The first time a Guest form drops to half its HP or less, two Crawling Pieces tear free and act at the end of that round.
  • It counts as Unholy. Flask of Holy Oil steps up a weapon's Effort against it; a Flask of holy water can be thrown at it. Let the players find this out by trying.
  • It wants to live, and it wants to get down the mountain. Tarnwick has three hundred people. It is patient. It will let a copy die to protect a better hidden one.

Fire at the table

The rules do not have a fire rule, so here is a ruling for this adventure. A burning brand is a Close weapon: STR check, Basic Effort d4, and it counts as fire. A lit Flask of lamp oil thrown into Close or Near is a DEX check; on success roll Basic Effort d4 and the target is burning: it takes d4 at the start of each round until it spends an Action smothering itself in snow. Track burning by hand. Spells such as Ember, Ember Psalm, Pyre and Kindle count as fire, and each non-healing spell still adds its 1 Horror. That cost should hurt here: fire is exactly what the heroes need, and magic is the quickest way to get it.

The priory

A tight cluster of grey stone round a square cloister yard, the snow in it waist-deep. Every building is a short walk through the cold from every other, and every walk is a moment someone is alone.

The people

Eight people are alive at the priory when the heroes arrive. Tick a box when someone is taken; it is only saved in your own browser.

Prior Severin Ashby

Proud, tired, charming. Ordered the dig. Wants the Ice Saint to be real, because he has already spent the money it would bring. Will lie to protect the priory's name.

Brother Lucan Tarn

The infirmarian. Clever, frightened, thorough. The first to understand. After scene 3 he decides nothing must leave the mountain, and he means nothing: he burns the rope bridge and bars the gate. He is never taken unless you choose; he is the dangerous human.

Brother Faustin Mourne

The old scribe, half-blind, gentle. Kept vigil over the Ice Saint. The Guest from the start. Tell: he reads small writing without his spectacles.

Brother Odo Wick

Cellarer and cook. Big, kind, laughs too loudly when scared. Being taken in the byre during scene 2.

Brother Dunstan Crane

A novice of fifteen who keeps the bell and the tower fire. He let the fire go out because Brother Faustin told him the Prior had forbidden it. He is ashamed and will not say so.

Canon Hildreth Vesper

Of the Order of the Vault, sent from Aubrecht to judge the Ice Saint. Cool, exact, unafraid. Secretly carries a Vault specimen jar holding a piece of the Ice Saint, cut off in autumn. The Order wants a live one for the Undervaults.

Ursel the Goatherd

A lay servant, fifty, hard as a boot. Trusts the goats more than the brothers. A natural ally, which is exactly why she is worth taking.

Jorun the Muleteer

Brought the Canon up and wants his pay and his road home. Quick to anger, quick to accuse, carries a crossbow. Will try to leave by the lower road at the worst possible moment.

Running the paranoia

The fights are short. The real game is the talking between them. A few habits keep it frightening:

  • Keep asking where everyone is. “Who goes to fetch the oil? Who stays with Brother Lucan? Who sleeps where?” Write it down where the players can see you writing.
  • Let the folk tests fail. The brothers have ideas: salt on the threshold, watching for breath in the cold, sharing the bread, the warmth of a rosary. None of them prove anything. The Coarse Salt Pouch says so itself. The Guest breathes, eats and prays very well.
  • Give true clues that only point at places. The Ashen Rosary warms when something supernatural is within Near, but not at whom. The Fragment of stained glass shows one image from the last day at one place: Faustin's hands on the coffin, and the ice moving under them. Both cost 1 Horror to use.
  • Let the players test each other. Once they learn the blood test in scene 3, they will want to use it on each other. Let them. Make it slow.
  • Borrowed Face is a gift. A hero who carries a book with Borrowed Face in it will be suspected the moment anyone reads it. Let the brothers find out.

The Taken Hero (optional, ask first)

Before play, ask privately whether one player would enjoy their hero being taken. If they say yes, and only then: when you choose, write in that hero's private notes on the table (only the owner and the GM can see them) “You were alone in the dark for too long. You are the Guest now. Nobody must know until you choose.” The player keeps playing their hero, keeps rolling honestly, and wins if the Guest gets down the mountain. Their blood fails the test. Never do this to a player who did not agree.

The Last Lamp

Target 12Gatehouse, chapel, refectoryArrival, introductions, the empty coffin

The snow has been coming sideways since noon. The mule is stumbling. Above you, the priory sits on its shelf of rock like a fist, and the bell tower is dark. The gate is shut. Drift is piled against it to the height of a man, and behind you, down the pass, you can hear the mountain moving.

Nobody answers the gate at first. The porter is gone. The heroes have to dig and force their way in while the storm closes the pass behind them.

The Pass Closes
Rounds
4, automatic

The mountain is moving. The road behind you will not be there long.

At 0 a snow shelf comes down across the lower road. Anyone still outside the gate takes d6 as the edge of it buries them. If the mule is not inside by then, it bolts down the pass with the supplies: the oil, candles and salt are lost.

Inside

Ursel lets them in, or finds them in the yard, with a goat-knife in her hand. She takes them to the refectory, where the survivors are gathered round the only fire they still trust. Let the players meet everyone in one room, at one table, so they can watch faces.

  • Prior Severin welcomes them graciously and explains nothing. The fire? An oversight. The porter? Gone down the road to Tarnwick, surely they passed him.
  • Brother Lucan asks too many questions about how long the heroes were on the road and whether they met anyone.
  • Canon Hildreth asks only whether the pass is closed. When told yes, she nods as if a decision has been made.
  • Brother Faustin pours the heroes hot wine and sits close to whichever one looks coldest.

The chapel

If the heroes ask about the Ice Saint, or wander, they find the chapel. The coffin is split from inside. The frozen smear on the floor runs to the vestry door and stops. The vestry is empty: robes on hooks, one of them missing.

The missing robe is the one Faustin wore at the vigil. It is in the icehouse, under the dead, with the porter: the first person the Guest killed, two nights ago, when he caught it trying the gate. The heroes cannot know that yet.

Leaving the scene

End with the first night. The Prior offers the guest cells in the dormitory. Ask each player where their hero sleeps, and with whom. Ask who keeps watch. Then let it be quiet for a while.

Rest: a Party rest is fine tonight, if the heroes keep a watch. If nobody watches, the Guest does not take a hero tonight, but tell the players nothing either way.

Brother Odo went out to the byre with a lamp because the goats were restless. A piece of Faustin was waiting in the hay. The heroes arrive halfway through. Odo's face is still Odo's; the rest of him is opening like a book.

Fire is the obvious answer, and the byre is a tinderbox. Start this countdown when anything burns. At 0 the roof comes down: anyone still inside takes d6, and everything left in the byre burns, including any Crawling Piece. The oil barrels go up too, which matters at Horror 14.

Running it

  • The Guest wants to escape, not win. If it is losing, it goes for the back wall, which is old timber, and the snow beyond.
  • Ursel arrives in round 2 from the loft, swinging a hay fork. She is human. Let a player wonder.
  • Count heads after the fight. Somebody is always late. Brother Faustin arrives last, in his night robe, carrying a candle, asking what happened.

After the fire

Brother Lucan kneels in the ash with tongs and lifts out something that is still moving. Now everybody knows: it can look like anyone. The Prior wants to pray. Jorun wants to leave. The Canon wants the thing in the tongs, very badly, and asks for it in a level voice.

If the Canon gets it, put it in her specimen jar. That raises the chance she is taken (she is 2 on the d6 table already) and gives you a second Guest to release at a bad moment.

The Cut

Target 13The glacier, the ruin of Ossk Varel, the barred gateWhat it is, the test, shut out

Brother Lucan says the answer is at the dig. The brothers' notes stop on the day they found the figure; something else was down there, and the Prior had the tunnel boarded up. Lucan cannot climb. The heroes can. Ask who stays behind. Jorun or the Canon will come if asked; Faustin offers, and should be refused, with a smile.

The glacier is blue inside. Your breath freezes on your scarves. At the top of the climb there is a lean-to of picks and planks, and a tunnel the brothers cut, boarded across. Someone has pulled the boards out from the inside.

What is in the ruin

Beyond the boards: a round chamber of black stone, older than anything the heroes have seen. Around the walls, in the ice, are people. Twelve of them, sitting in a ring, facing inward, dressed in nothing anyone can name. In front of each one is a stain in the ice, bright red, and the stains are not frozen. They are still wet after all these centuries.

Carved on the centre stone: a row of figures, each one holding out a cut hand over a white field. Under most hands the drops lie still. Under two, the drops have stood up and are running towards each other.

Learning the test

Any of these teaches it:

  • An INT check against the Target to read the carving and the stains together.
  • Speak with the Dead or Confession of the Dead on one of the ring: “Which of you were still yourselves?” “The ones whose blood went cold.”
  • Simply watching their own blood: any hero who is cut on the climb sees it freeze black on the ice in moments.

The test. Human blood dropped on snow freezes into a dark crust within a breath. The Guest's blood does not freeze. It stays bright and wet, and then it slides across the snow towards the nearest other part of the Guest. A test can expose two at once. It needs snow, a blade and light to see by.

The Chapter House

Target 12The Chapter House, everyone presentThe test, the reveal, a fight among the bound

Everyone alive goes into the Chapter House. Lucan bars the door. He has rope and insists everyone is tied to the stone benches by one wrist, heroes included, except whoever does the testing. A bucket of snow sits by the hearth. The hearth is the only light.

Roll for the second Guest now if the Horror has not reached 7.

Everyone left alive, on stone benches, one wrist each tied to the iron rings that once held the brothers' book-chains. The fire spits. Brother Lucan holds out a knife, handle first. “I do not trust my own hand,” he says. “Someone else must cut me first.”

At 0 the fire is out. The only light is what the heroes carry; without light the test cannot be read at all. At Horror 14 or more, start it at 4.

The test, one person at a time
Blood on snow
Cost

Check

Cut, drip, read. With good light, a Normal WIS check against the Target. In poor light, Hard. A success reads the blood clearly. A failure means it is smeared or unclear: test that person again. A hero's natural 1 also smears it, and adds its 1 Horror as usual.

Who goes first

Let the players choose the order and argue about it. The NPCs push: the Prior insists on going last, as befits his office. Jorun insists the heroes go first since they were away. The Canon asks quietly that her jar not be opened. Lucan goes first if allowed, and is human.

The reveal

The Guest does not wait to be tested. When the knife is one person away from a taken one, or when the countdown reaches 2, it unveils. If the second Guest's blood is tested first, Faustin's drop slides across the snow to it, and both unveil together.

Running it

  • Tied heroes are still in the fight: cutting free takes a Move with a blade, or an Easy STR check to tear the old ring from the wall.
  • The hearth is right there. Pushing a Guest form into it counts as fire: on a STR success, roll Basic d4 and it is burning.
  • Protect the bound. Ask who unties whom first. It says a lot about trust.
  • When the fight ends, whatever escaped (pieces not burned, a Guest that fled) goes down through the kitchen trapdoor. Let the heroes find the trail: a smear across the refectory floor and the trapdoor open.

Rest: a short, wary Party rest is allowed now, but only if the heroes bar the kitchen. If they rest, start scene 5's countdown 2 lower.

The Icehouse

Target 14Below the kitchen, the bell towerThe finale: burn it, warn the valley

The ladder goes down into blue cold. The brothers keep their winter dead here, wrapped and stacked like cordwood until the ground softens. The stacks have been pulled apart. At the far end, where the old meltwater channel drains away under the mountain, something has made itself out of them. It is very busy. It is digging.

Everything left of the Guest has gone below and gathered into one body, built from the dead. It is boring along the meltwater channel, which runs under the mountain and comes out in the spring above Tarnwick. If it reaches the village, the heroes have lost.

Obstacle · optional
The Three Fires
Progress

Check

Clearing and lighting all three on the bell tower tells Tarnwick: plague, stay away. Whatever happens below, nobody from the valley comes up. If the Guest gets away, the village has warning; it is Brother Dunstan's idea, if he lives.

Ways to win

  • Burn it. Finish Fire the Icehouse and get out. The cleanest ending, and the priory's kitchen and refectory go with it.
  • Kill it with fire. Bring it to 0 HP with fire, or bring it to 0 and burn the remains before the end of the next round.
  • Close the channel. Psalm of Ruin brings down the channel roof (1 Horror). This buries it; it does not end it. Spring will come. Say so.
  • Split the party. Some fight below, some climb the tower to light the three fires. That is the best plan and the most frightening one, because nobody can see the others.

The last image

Whatever happens, end on two survivors sitting in the snow outside the burning building, watching each other across the fire. One of them offers the other a drink. Ask the player whether their hero takes it. Then stop.

Rewards

Award catalogue loot as you see fit. Things that belong on this mountain:

  • The Ashen Rosary (relic, 1 Horror): Prior Severin's, on his body or his desk. It warms near something supernatural, as it did all winter.
  • The Ashen Psalter (grimoire, Fire), in the scriptorium, hidden behind the account books. Ember, Kindle and Pillar of Ash. Finding it in scene 1 or 2 makes the whole adventure a question about how much Horror the party will spend on fire.
  • Physician's satchel from the infirmary.
  • Vault specimen jar, the Canon's. Empty, if the players are lucky.
  • Inquisitor's warrant or Letter of safe passage in the Canon's papers, for whoever survives to explain all this in Aubrecht.

Setting it up on the Sacrament RPG Table

  • Make one scene per chapter above and choose Prepare to build each privately. Show scene 1 when play starts.
  • Put each enemy on as a token with its HP and the description in italics above as its public text; put the rest in its private GM notes. Use Hide the numbers from players on every Guest form. Nobody should know how close it is to breaking.
  • Put obstacles on as cards with Effort progress, and countdowns as cards with a timer. Leave the ones marked paused paused until the fiction starts them.
  • Keep Crawling Pieces and the Sleeper in the Wall hidden until they appear. Hidden cards are not sent to players at all.
  • Set the Target per scene by hand when the scene goes live: the Target belongs to the table, not to the scene.
  • The NPCs are not on the table as tokens unless you want them there. If you add them, give them no numbers. Players should not be able to tell a monster from a monk by its card.
