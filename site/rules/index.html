Sacrament

The Basic Rules

We can reach for more power. What will we awaken? One number for the scene, one die to find out whether you succeed, one die to find out how much. The Looming Horror is what you pay for reaching further than you should. These rules are still being tested at the table, so expect numbers to move.

1 The basics

Sacrament is played by a GM, who describes the world and speaks for everyone in it, and two to five players, who each play one hero. You need a d20 and one each of d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12. The sheet and the table on this site do the arithmetic, but the game plays at a kitchen table with the dice alone.

The GM describes a situation, the players say what they do, and when the outcome is in doubt, someone makes a check.

2 Checks

Roll a d20, add a score, and meet or beat the scene’s Target Number (TN). A natural 20 always succeeds and is a critical. A natural 1 always fails. The GM sets one TN for the scene, says it aloud, and leaves it visible until the scene ends.

Easy and Hard. The GM may call one action Easy (TN −3) or Hard (TN +3), saying so before the roll. They never stack. A TN stays between 5 and 19, so a natural 20 is always needed for the impossible, and a natural 1 always ruins the trivial.

There are six scores. STR is melee, lifting, climbing and raw force. DEX is shooting, stealth, locks, dodging. CON is endurance, and with armour it is your Defence. INT is spells, lore and investigation. WIS is perception, instinct, mending and tending the dying. CHA is persuasion, leadership and fear. Your modifier is the score plus any bonus, never more than +10. A hero starts with six points to spend, so a specialist begins at +3: about a 60% chance against TN 12. Nothing is automatic; nothing is hopeless.

When a check fails, the story does not stop. The GM says what it costs, or what goes wrong, and play moves on.

3 Effort and Hearts

Not every success needs a number. If the amount does not matter, the success just happens. When it does matter (damage, progress, healing), roll an Effort die.

Basic d4Bare hands, an improvised weapon, a clumsy tool.
Weapons d6Ordinary melee weapons, bows, crossbows, slings and tools used as weapons.
Guns d8Guns, gunpowder weapons and similar devices.
Magic d10Any spell or prayer that does harm, and a relic blade.
Ultimate d12A once-a-scene blow from a relic or the Book of Dust, and what a natural 20 adds.

Each hero has four points of Effort to put into those five kinds. Whatever the die shows, you add your points in that kind: a hero with +3 Weapons rolls d6+3 with a spear. Healing dice are not changed by Effort.

A critical (natural 20) rolls the Effort die as usual and adds an ultimate d12 on top, plus your Ultimate points. Some things step the die up (holy oil against the unquiet) or down (a plain weapon against armour) one size, never past d4 or d12.

Hearts

A Heart is 10 points. Health is counted in Hearts, and so is progress on a hard task. A hero starts with 1 Heart (10 points) and gains more from gear (the plate harness carries you through a blow: +1 Heart). Foes are given in Hearts.

A long or dangerous task is an Obstacle of 1 (a quick hindrance), 2 (demanding) or 3 (formidable) Hearts. Each success rolls an Effort die and adds it to the progress; filling the Hearts finishes it. A failure costs something: a noise, a danger, or 1d4 damage that nothing reduces. A critical adds a d12. Use an Obstacle only when the tension is worth a few rolls: a locked door in a quiet cellar is just a roll, or no roll.

4 Your hero

  • Scores. Spend 6 points on STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS and CHA (no more than 6 in one), and 4 points of Effort on Basic, Weapons, Guns, Magic and Ultimate.
  • Health. 1 Heart (10 points), more from gear.
  • Kit: ten equipped, ten carried. Every item says how many slots it takes: a hatchet 1, a boar spear 2, a plate harness 3, and every grimoire 3. You can equip ten slots (weapons, armour, relics, grimoires: the things you wear, wield or have ready) and carry ten more in the pack (supplies and spares). Only equipped gear works; supplies work from the pack. Swapping a spare in is free between turns, or a Move in a fight. A starting kit has no more than three relics and grimoires.
  • Defence is your CON, plus armour and shield (a few foes attack your WIS instead, with no armour) (armour and shield never add more than +5; Defence never passes +10). A foe must roll 10 + your Defence to hit you.
  • A vow. One sentence the GM may call on.

Equipment choices. Equip one body armour, one helmet and one shield. Heavy two-handed melee weapons roll Weapons Effort plus your Weapons bonus plus 2 damage. Reach weapons keep normal Weapons Effort and their reach or control ability. Two-handed weapons require your shield to be in the pack. Spare equipment still takes slots; moving it during a fight takes a Move.

Progression comes from what you find. After an adventure each hero gets a Discovery: a relic, a grimoire page, a weapon with a history, or a Mark (+1 to one score, to a base of +3 at most). There are no levels and no abilities to learn: what a hero can do is what they carry.

Magic is in objects. A grimoire takes three slots and holds three spells; a relic does one dangerous thing. Neither is learned: you cast from the book you carry. Lose the book and you lose the spells. A new spell is a new item.

5 Turns

Rounds go clockwise round the table, then the GM acts for every foe. On your turn you have one Move and one Action. When a check fails, you may push once for a second Action at 1 Horror: the same thing again or something else. The push costs 1, and the second Action then costs whatever it normally costs. Nothing is charged twice for the same thing, and a pushed Action cannot be pushed again.

Strike or shootA STR check in melee, a DEX check at range. On a success roll your weapon’s Effort die and add your Effort points: that is the damage.
Cast or use a relicThe check and die the item names. The Horror cost is paid as you roll.
AssistAn ally’s next check before your next turn is Easy (−3).
GuardUntil your next turn, your Defence is +2.
TendA WIS check on a dying or hurt ally in reach.
DashMove up to two bands instead of one (not in a harness).

Distance bands

There is no grid. Everyone is in one of four bands from the person acting: Engaged (touching), Close (a few strides), Far (across the room) and Distant (out of the fight). A Move changes your band by one. Melee needs Engaged; a thrown thing or a reach weapon needs Close; bows, crossbows and spells reach Far. The GM decides who is where.

6 Combat

Your Strike or spell is a check against the scene TN; on a success roll the Effort die and take that many points from the foe. An armoured foe slows a plain weapon: one whose Effort is under d8 (not a relic, not a spell) steps down one size.

On the GM’s turn each foe has a Move and an Action. When a foe attacks, the GM rolls a d20 for it against 10 + your Defence. The danger of the scene adds to the roll: +1 at Dangerous (TN 12), +3 at Deadly (14), +5 at Doomed (16), nothing at Wary (10). (A table may let the player tap the dice instead; the numbers are the same.) At the digital table the GM sets each monster’s attack bonus (put the scene’s danger in it) and the table rolls d20 + that bonus against 10 + your Defence; on a hit the GM picks the Effort and it comes off your hit points at once. A hit on a hero who is down takes a round off the countdown instead.

It does not reach 10 + your DefenceIt misses, or you slip it.
It reaches itRoll its Effort die. You lose that many points.
Natural 20 / natural 1A 20 always hits. A 1 always misses.
DazedA Dazed foe rolls 3 less on its next attack.

When nothing hostile is awake, the scene ends. Stable heroes wake with 1 point, and anything “once a scene” is ready again.

7 Hurt, dying and healing

At 0 points you fall and are dying. Roll a d4: that is your countdown, in rounds. Write it where everyone can see it. At the digital table the d4 is rolled for you and the countdown sits on your token; the GM marks you stable after a Tend.

  • Timing. At the end of every round, after the foes have acted, each dying hero’s countdown drops by 1. At 0 they are dead.
  • Being hit again. A blow that gets through on a dying hero takes a round off the countdown instead of rolling damage.
  • Tending. An ally in reach uses an Action and makes a WIS check (Easy with bandages). On a success the hero is stable: the countdown stops and they stay at 0 until healed or the scene ends. A critical also recovers 1d4.
  • Healing. Any healing of 1 or more stands a dying or stable hero up with that many points. A stable hero who is hurt again is dying again and rolls a new d4.

Each source of healing works once per person per rest, unless that person is dying or down:

Rest at a hearth1 Heart (10 points).
Linen bandagesd6, a WIS check.
Mend (the Litany)d8, a WIS check, 1 Horror.
Saint’s knuckleboned8, no roll, 1 Horror.

Death Bargains are set aside for now. They may return as an optional module for tables that want a way back from death at a cost.

8 The Looming Horror

At the Sacrament RPG Table: the Rising Horror

The table keeps one meter for everyone, from 0 to 21, in its top bar. Every time a hero casts a spell or uses a relic or other item that tempts the Horror, the meter rises by that thing’s Horror amount, by itself, and the activity feed says so. It rises whether the check succeeds or fails: using it is what tempts the Horror. An amount of 0 adds nothing.

Two more things raise it by 1: a hero’s d20 showing a natural 1 (a monster’s does not count), and a push. After a failed check the table offers the hero a push: they take a second Action at once, and the meter rises by 1. A push is once a turn, and only after a failure.

Most amounts are a fixed number. Some are rolled when the thing is used, such as d4+2 or d6, and the feed shows the roll. Reading the Grimoire with no title rolls a d4 to choose the spell, and the reader casts whichever comes up, at that spell’s own amount.

The meter has thresholds at 7, 14 and 21. Passing one is announced in the feed (“The Horror stirs”, “The Horror draws near”) and the GM is alerted; the GM decides what happens. At 21 the Horror has arrived: everyone is told, and the meter stays at 21 until the GM lowers or resets it. It never falls by itself. Only the GM moves it by hand or takes back a rise. The GM’s Party rest (a rest in a safe place) heals every living hero 1 Heart and lowers it by a d6 for every two living heroes.

The rest of this section is the draft rules the table was designed from. Where they differ from the table above, the table is how it plays now.

One shared track, 0 to 21, written where everyone can see it. It has three thresholds: 9 The Stirring, 15 The Turning and 21 The Arrival. Horror rises only by voluntary choices. Failing a check does not add it, and neither does taking damage.

A Minor spell or prayer+1
A Greater working+2
An Ultimate working+3
A relicAs the relic says, +1 to +3.
A push+1

When it is paid. At the moment you roll for the thing (or, for something with no roll, the moment you declare it), whether the roll succeeds or fails. If you are refused before the roll (not allowed, used up, the Arrival has stopped all magic), nothing is paid. A push is paid when you take the second Action.

Thresholds

  1. The GM says aloud: “The ladder reaches 9: the Stirring.”
  2. At the end of the Action that crossed it, before the next turn, roll the aftershock d6 for that threshold.
  3. A threshold fires once. It fires again only after the track has fallen below it and climbed back.

Edge cases

  • Several thresholds at once. Resolve each in ascending order, one aftershock roll each, after the action that crossed them.
  • Falling below and crossing again. Resting from 11 to 7 re-arms the 9. Rising to 9 later fires it again; staying at or above 9 does not.
  • At 21. The track does not rise further. Each point that would is paid by whoever caused it as Strain: 2 damage that nothing reduces. A Minor spell costs its caster 2; an Ultimate working, 6. Magic and pushing are never free, and a hero may decline.
  • The Arrival can forbid magic (result 3). Nothing is attempted, so nothing is paid.
  • Healing magic outside combat costs Horror exactly as in combat, and works once per person per rest. A second casting on the same person heals nothing and still costs.
  • Several heroes at once. Every cost is applied to the one track in the order it happens (clockwise within a round). Costs paid in the same action are added first; thresholds are resolved after the whole action.

Resting

Only a party rest in a safe place lowers the track. A safe place (a hearth) is marked by the GM: somewhere the party can hold for a watch (a door that bars, hallowed ground, a locked reliquary room) with nothing hostile awake that knows they are there. A hearth restores once. A rest takes one whole watch; if it is interrupted, nothing is restored and the hearth is still there.

A rest heals every living hero 1 Heart, refreshes the once-per-rest healing and takes a d6 off the track for every two living heroes (1 to 2 heroes: 1d6; 3 to 4: 2d6; 5 to 6: 3d6). The Turning’s “troubled night” removes the reduction once, but not the healing. The Arrival’s “dawn does not come” forbids resting there.

9 Aftershocks

Roll a d6 at the end of the Action that crossed the threshold. Rows marked kept are from the earlier rules word for word; adapted rows changed to fit these rules.

10 Items, foes and heroes

Every item says how many slots it takes, when it is used, the roll it needs, the Effort die it rolls and the Horror it adds. What it lets you do is the point; the numbers are the smaller part.

Foes

Hearts are 10 points each. A foe rolls its Effort die when its attack gets through your Defence.

11 An example of play

Four heroes in the flooded crypt at Odrin’s Rest. The scene is Dangerous: TN 12.

Ordinary check. Wren picks the lock on the sacristy door. The GM calls it Easy, because the lock is old: TN 9. Wren rolls a d20 and adds her DEX (+3): 7 + 3 = 10. She opens it, and the GM does not ask for an Effort die. Nothing about the amount matters.

Aldric hooks a gaunt penitent with the bill hook. His STR is +3 and he rolls an 11 for 14: a success. Rather than damage it, he chooses the hook’s other effect and drags it from Close to Engaged, where the shield can reach it. No Effort die: the amount does not matter.

Ottoline casts Ember at the penitent from her grimoire: an INT check, +3, a 15 for 18. Success. Ember is a Minor spell, so the track goes from 0 to 1, and it would have gone to 1 if she had missed. She rolls d10 for the flame, a 5, and adds her Magic Effort (+3): 8. The penitent has 10 points, so it is at 2.

The sexton surges out of the font, 2 Hearts and armoured. Ottoline pushes for a second Action (+1 Horror, now 2) and casts Unmake at it: +2 Horror (now 4). A spell is not slowed by armour. She rolls the d10, a 7, and adds +3: 10. The sexton, at 20, is at 10, and Dazed.

Defence. The sexton lunges at Maren. Her Defence is her CON (+2) and her gambeson (+2): 4, so the sexton must roll 14. The GM rolls a d20 for it: a 14, and the scene adds 1 for 15. It hits. The sexton rolls its d10: an 8. Maren, at 10, is at 2. The hound’s bite next round takes the rest. She is dying, and rolls a d4: a 2.

At the end of the round the countdown falls to 1. Wren runs to her and uses her Action to tend: a WIS check with bandages is Easy, TN 9. She rolls a 14 and adds +1. Maren is stable, at 0. The Horror is at 4; a rest at the sacristy will take 2d6 off it.

12 For the GM

Sizing a fight. Add up the foes’ Hearts and compare with the number of heroes.

Skirmish½ a Heart per hero. Almost nothing.
Standard1 per hero. About a quarter of the party’s health; sometimes someone falls.
Hard1½ per hero. About half; someone usually falls.
Deadly1¾ or more per hero. Leave room to retreat.

Timers. To keep the pressure on, set a timer: a named count (“the bell tolls”, 4). It falls by one at the end of every round, or when you take one off by hand. At 0, the thing happens; a timer can be set to start again at 0, for something that keeps coming. Players see every timer, and only the GM changes them.

These come from a crude simulation, not from play. Treat them as a starting point to correct at the table.

Setting the TN. Start every scene at Wary or Dangerous. Raise it for a place or a foe that is plainly better than the party. Leave it alone when the party is clever, and reward cleverness with an Easy.

Aiming the Horror. Do not add Horror yourself. Offer choices that tempt: a locked door that magic opens in a moment, a wounded friend, a foe iron will not stop. The Horror is the price players choose to pay for convenience. Your job is to make sure the convenient thing is tempting and the consequence is real.
