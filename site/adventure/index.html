Playtest

The Sexton’s Ledger

An early playtest of Sacrament, kept as a record of how the game developed.

Earlier rules. This adventure used an older turn and damage system. For a game today, use the player guide and GM guide. The notes below preserve what happened in that playtest.

The adventure is the one from the rules page, grown into five scenes. The characters are built with the real character rules, the adversaries come from the VTT’s bestiary, and every die is rolled by a program that follows the rules as written. Where the rules say nothing, it makes the plainest guess and says so. Then it ran the whole adventure 3,000 more times to see what usually happens.

This is the second round. The first playtest found five serious problems (F1 to F5) and the rules were changed to fix them. The page below plays the adventure under the new rules and puts the old numbers beside the new ones.

The verdict

Before the changes the characters could not lose: in 88% of fights the party finished the adversaries before one of them acted, and the whole adventure cost the party about 0.2 harm out of 36. Now fights last about 1.9 rounds, the adversaries make about 14.1 attacks, the party takes about 8.3 harm, and the Looming Horror climbs past the first milestone in 96% of runs. Somebody falls to 12 harm in about 3% of runs. That is a game you can lose, which was the point.

  1. F1. Six actions against one. Fixed: three actions a turn, and adversaries get several attacks.
  2. F2. Armour and adversaries cancel. Fixed: adversaries roll an extra die and attack more often.
  3. F3. Weapons beat spells. Fixed: damage spells roll tokens plus one dice, and a Strike costs a token more for each weapon level above 2.
  4. F4. The Looming Horror never rises. Fixed: Wounds and failed checks raise it too.
  5. F5. Healing and resting wipe the slate. Fixed: magic heals each character once between rests, a rest heals one Wound, and the party cleanses once per rest.

What the simulation had to guess
  • A spell that does damage needs an attack check like a Strike. It adds Spirit for the Cantor and Mind for the Wanderer, and the spell adds its level.
  • An adversary makes the number of attacks listed in the bestiary in its turn, and the GM picks a target for each one at random among the characters still standing (and the NPC).
  • The GM chooses targets at random among the characters who are still standing. The rules give no targeting guidance.
  • A character at 12 harm is down and takes no turns. At the start of each following round they roll the dying d6 (Death Bargain on 1-2, stable on 3-5, revive with 1 Wound on a 6). Bargain quests, rerolls and attribute penalties are not modelled, only the harm and Looming Horror effects. A dead character stays dead.
  • Under the new rules a natural 20 on an attack check always hits and rolls damage twice. A natural 1 always misses.
  • Adversary armour (the VTT has it) subtracts its d6 from damage like player armour.
  • Tokens refill at the start of every round and between fights. The rules do not say what tokens mean outside a fight.
  • A Horror Token can be spent once per round, and only if the Looming Horror is above 0.

The five fixes

Each says what the problem was, what changed, and what the numbers say now.

F1 · Fixed

Six actions against one

Was: a character could Strike up to six times a turn while an adversary attacked once, so 88% of fights ended before the adversaries acted. Now: a turn is at most 3 actions (reactions do not count), and every adversary has an attacks number: the rats 1, the Grave Hound 2, the sexton 3. Their HP is about four times what it was (rats 24, hound 32, sexton 40). Result: fights run about 1.9 rounds, the adversaries make about 14.1 attacks instead of 0.3, and a fight ends before the adversaries act in 0% of cases.

F2 · Fixed

Armour and adversary damage cancel out

Was: level 2 armour rolled as much as a whole adversary attack. Now: the bestiary’s attackers roll one die more (2d6 to 3d6 at this level) and attack up to three times. One 2d6 hit still gets past level 2 armour 44% of the time, and with Guard 16%, but a turn is two or three hits and the party takes about 8.3 harm over the adventure. Armour is still worth having. It is no longer a wall.

F3 · Fixed

Weapons beat spells

Was: every damage spell worked out at 3.5 damage a token, and a weapon of level 2 or more beat it, up to 17.5 a token for a level 5 weapon. Now: a damage spell rolls tokens plus one dice (Firebolt 3d6 for 2 tokens, Arcane Cataclysm 6d6 for 5), and a Strike costs 1 token more for each weapon level above 2 (a level 3 weapon costs 2, a level 5 weapon 4). Per token, the table below now runs about 4.2 to 5.3 for every spell and for level 3 to 5 weapons. A level 2 weapon is still 7 a token, which is fine: it is a plain weapon, and spells keep their real edges of ignoring armour and hitting several enemies, at the price of Looming Horror. The three characters here carry level 1 and 2 weapons, so the numbers for the adventure did not move.

F4 · Fixed

The Looming Horror barely moved

Was: it peaked at 1.6 and never reached 9 in 3,000 runs, and the GM had a Horror Token 0 times an adventure. Now: it also rises by 1 when a character fills a Wound and by 1 when a character fails a check that is not an attack. It peaks at 13.8, it reaches 9 in 96% of runs, and the GM can spend about 3.2 Horror Tokens an adventure. The milestones stay at 9, 15 and 21. The d6 aftershock tables for them are still unwritten (see below).

F5 · Fixed

Healing and cleansing wiped the slate

Was: Litany of Mending could be cast over and over, a safe rest removed the Horror it made, and every rest started clean. Now: magical healing mends each character once between rests; a rest (a night somewhere safe, not a breather in a porch) heals 1 Wound and refills tokens; and a rest in a safe place rolls a d6 to lower the ladder. In the run above Osric ends the adventure still carrying harm, and the ladder is still high after the morning rest instead of 0. One thing is still unclear: what a character who falls to 12 harm can do (see below).

Still open

F6 · Moderate

Success has no mechanics

A failed check now costs 1 Looming Horror, which gives failure a price. Nothing says what a success earns, how to set a difficulty, or what else failure can cost.

Fix: a short table of gains for success and costs for failure.

F7 · Moderate

Reactions need tokens nobody says to keep

Interpose and Arcane Ward are free of the three-action limit but still cost a token on someone else’s turn. They only work if a character stopped with a token to spare. The three-action limit makes that easier, since most characters finish a turn with tokens left, but the rules should say so.

Fix: say that tokens left at the end of your turn can be spent on reactions.

F8 · Moderate

NPCs have no rules

Leofwyn is 14 HP and no armour, up from 6, because at 6 the sexton killed her in nearly every run. Even at 14 she dies in 39% of runs, because the GM picks targets at random and she is the weakest. Nothing in the rules says what an NPC can do or what protecting one costs.

Fix: an NPC block with HP, an armour level and one action.

What works

  • Checks. A d20 plus a bonus against a difficulty from Easy 8 to Legendary 22 is quick, readable and well spread (table above).
  • The three bands of distance (Melee, Near, Far) are easy to use and removed all the measuring.
  • Tokens as the only budget make every turn a choice, and the idea of keeping one back for a reaction is a good one.
  • Popcorn order let the table decide who goes next, and it made the fights read like a story.
  • Armour as dice you roll is quick and satisfying, and now it has a counterweight (F2).
  • Harm as 12 boxes in 3 Wounds is clear at a glance and makes healing easy to read.
  • Adversaries that never roll to hit speed play up. The player rolls their armour and that is the whole exchange.
  • The Looming Horror as a shared ladder is a strong idea, and with F4 fixed it now drives the pace of the session.

What the rules do not say

The simulation had to guess each of these, and a real table would argue about them.

  • Dying and the Death Bargain table are written on the rules page and the simulation rolls the dying d6 each round, but it only models the harm and Looming Horror effects of a bargain, not the quests. The milestone aftershock tables are not rolled at all.
  • Who an adversary chooses to attack. The number of attacks is now in the bestiary, but the choice is the GM’s alone.
  • What a condition is (Absolution clears “one condition”), or what deafened, staggered or rooted do.
  • How tokens work outside a fight. (Healing and resting outside one are now written down: once per rest.)
  • What surprise does. The simulation let the adversaries go first when the party failed the first scene.

How this was done

A program plays the adventure using the real rules engine and the real gear and ability library, so any change to the rules would show up here. The page shows one fixed run (seed 14). The tables are the average of 3,000 runs of the whole adventure at different seeds for each kind of player, and the same for each rule-change row. The checks and damage tables are calculated exactly, not sampled. The code lives in the project under scripts/playtest, and this page is rebuilt from it by running one command whenever the rules change. The “before” numbers were saved from the first playtest; the adversaries’ HP, damage and attacks were tuned with the simulator until fights lasted about two rounds and the party took roughly a Wound or two per fight.
