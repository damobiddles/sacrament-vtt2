# Sacrament: Hearts. Core rules (draft v0.1, for a first human playtest)

*A simpler Sacrament. One number for the scene, one die to find out whether you succeed, one die to find out how much. The Looming Horror stays: it is what you pay for reaching further than you should.*

These are the rules the site runs on, written as a draft for a first human playtest. Where a rule is carried over from the earlier game it says **(kept)**; where it is new or changed it says **(new)** or **(changed)**. Every number is a proposal.

> **We can reach for more power. What will we awaken?**

## 1. What you need

A GM and two to five players. A d20 and one each of d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12. A pencil. The website has a character sheet and a table tool that do the arithmetic, but the game plays at a kitchen table with the dice alone.

## 2. Checks (new)

When a result is in doubt, **roll a d20, add a score, and meet or beat the scene's Target Number (TN)**. A natural 20 always succeeds and is a *critical*. A natural 1 always fails.

The GM sets **one TN for the scene** and says it aloud, and it stays visible to everyone until the scene ends.

| Scene | TN | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| Wary | 10 | A tense place with room to breathe. |
| Dangerous | 12 | The usual setting for a fight or a hard task. |
| Deadly | 14 | Something here is far better at this than you are. |
| Doomed | 16 | The Turning has begun. Keep it for the worst of the night. |

**Easy and Hard.** The GM may call one action **Easy (TN −3)** or **Hard (TN +3)**, saying so *before* the roll. Easy and Hard never stack. A TN never goes below 5 or above 19, so a natural 20 is always needed for the impossible and a natural 1 always ruins the trivial.

**Scores.** There are six: **STR** (melee, lifting, climbing), **DEX** (shooting, stealth, locks, dodging), **CON** (endurance, and with armour your Defence), **INT** (spells, lore, investigation), **WIS** (perception, instinct, mending, tending the dying) and **CHA** (persuasion, leadership, fear). Your modifier is the score plus any bonus from a relic, and is never more than **+10**.

A starting hero has a best score of +2, so about a 55% chance against TN 12. Nothing is automatic; nothing is hopeless.

**When a check fails**, the story does not stop. The GM says what it costs or what goes wrong.

## 3. Effort (new)

Not every success needs a number. *If the amount does not matter, the success just happens.* When it does matter (how much damage, how much of a task is done, how far someone is healed), **roll an Effort die**.

| Effort die | What it stands for |
|---|---|
| **Basic d4** | Bare hands, an improvised weapon, a clumsy tool. |
| **Weapons d6** | Swords, axes, spears, bill hooks, cudgels, a craftsman’s tool. |
| **Ranged d8** | A bow or a crossbow. |
| **Magic d10** | Any spell or prayer that does harm, and a relic blade. |
| **Ultimate d12** | A once-a-scene blow from a relic or the Book of Dust, and what a natural 20 adds. |

Each hero has **four points of Effort** to put into those five kinds, and **adds their points in that kind** to the Effort die they roll (a hero with +3 Weapons rolls d6+3 with a spear). Healing dice are not changed by Effort. A **critical** (natural 20) rolls the Effort die as usual and **adds an ultimate d12** on top, plus the hero’s Ultimate points. An item says which die it uses. Some things step the die **up** (holy oil against the unquiet) or **down** (a plain weapon against armour) one size, never past d4 or d12.

### Hearts

A **Heart is 10 points**. Health is counted in Hearts, and so is progress on a hard task.

- **Health.** A hero starts with **1 Heart (10 points)** and gains more from gear (the plate harness: +1 Heart). Foes are given in Hearts: a gaunt penitent is 1, a drowned sexton 2, a relic-bound knight 3, the Hollow Bishop 5.
- **Obstacles.** A long or dangerous task is given a number of Hearts (1 is a quick hindrance, 2 demanding, 3 formidable). Each success rolls an Effort die and adds it to the task’s progress; filling the Hearts finishes it. A **failure costs something**: the GM adds a noise, a danger, or 1d4 damage that nothing reduces. A critical adds a d12.

*Use an Obstacle only when the task is tense and the tension is worth a few rolls. A locked door in a quiet cellar is just a roll, or no roll.*

## 4. Your hero

- **Scores.** Spend **6 points** on STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS and CHA (no more than 6 in one), and **4 points of Effort** on Basic, Weapons, Ranged, Magic and Ultimate.
- **Health.** 1 Heart (10 points), more from gear.
- **Kit.** **Ten equipped, ten carried.** Every item says how many slots it takes: a dagger 1, a boar spear 2, a plate harness 3, every grimoire 3. You can *equip* ten slots (weapons, armour, relics, grimoires) and *carry* ten more in the pack (supplies and spares); there is no “encumbered”. Only equipped gear works; supplies work from the pack. Swapping a spare in is free between turns, or a Move in a fight. Choose a **Calling** for a ready-made kit, or build your own with no more than three relics and grimoires.
- **Defence** is your CON, plus your armour and shield (armour and shield never add more than +5; Defence never passes +10). A few foes attack your WIS instead, with no armour. A foe must roll **10 + your Defence** to hit you.
- **A vow.** One sentence. The GM may call on it.

**Progression** (new) comes from what you find, not what you spend. At the end of an adventure the GM gives each hero a **Discovery**: a relic, a grimoire page, a weapon with a history, or a **Mark** (+1 to one score, to a base of +3 at most). You do not gain levels, learn abilities or increase your health by experience. A Heart can be won from a rare relic.

**Equipment choices.** Every grimoire takes three slots. Heavy two-handed melee weapons add +2 damage to Weapons Effort, including when a natural 20 upgrades the roll to Ultimate. Reach/control weapons keep normal damage and their stated benefit. Using a two-handed weapon requires putting the shield in the pack. Only one body armour, one helmet and one shield may contribute; spare armour belongs in the pack. The VTT enforces these equipment restrictions and the heavy damage bonus.

## 5. The turn (changed)

Rounds go **clockwise** round the table, beginning with the player to the GM’s left (or whoever last did something brave). Then the GM acts for every foe.

On your turn you have:

- **One Move.** Change your distance band by one (see below). Free speech, dropping or drawing an item, and small interactions come with it.
- **One Action.** Strike, shoot, cast, use a relic, Assist, Guard, Tend, Dash, or use an item that says Action.
- **Push (once per turn).** Take a **second Action** for **1 Horror**. The push costs 1 and the second Action then costs whatever it normally costs; nothing is charged twice for the same thing.

Items may be **Free** (usable once per turn without an Action), **Move** or **Action**, as they say.

**Standard Actions**

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Strike or shoot** | A STR check in melee, a DEX check at range. On a success roll your weapon’s Effort die and add your Effort points: that is the damage. |
| **Cast or use a relic** | The check and die the item names. The Horror cost is paid as you roll. |
| **Assist** | Help an ally: their next check before your next turn is Easy (−3). |
| **Guard** | Until your next turn, your Defence is +2. |
| **Tend** | A WIS check on a dying or hurt ally in reach. See §7. |
| **Dash** | Move up to two bands instead of one (not in a harness). |

### Distance bands (new)

There is no grid. Everyone is in one of four bands from the person acting: **Engaged** (touching), **Close** (a few strides), **Far** (across the room) and **Distant** (out of the fight). A Move changes your band by one, towards or away from one thing you name. Melee needs Engaged; a thrown thing or a reach weapon needs Close; bows, crossbows and spells reach Far. The GM decides who is where, and may call a band “as drawn on the map”.

## 6. Combat (new)

**Your attacks.** A Strike or spell is a check against the scene TN. On a success, roll your Effort die and take that many points from the foe. A foe at 0 is down (a named foe may be left to die or to flee, at the GM’s word).

**Armoured foes.** A foe marked *armoured* slows a plain weapon: a weapon whose Effort is under d8 (not a relic, not a spell) steps down one size against it.

**Their attacks.** On the GM’s turn each foe has a Move and an Action. When a foe attacks, **the GM rolls a d20 for it against 10 + your Defence**. The danger of the scene adds to the roll: +1 at Dangerous (TN 12), +3 at Deadly (14), +5 at Doomed (16), nothing at Wary (10). A table may let the player tap the dice instead; the numbers are the same.

- **Below 10 + your Defence:** it misses, or you slip it.
- **At or above it:** roll its Effort die. You lose that many points.
- **Natural 20 / natural 1:** a 20 always hits; a 1 always misses.

**Dazed.** A Dazed foe rolls 3 less on its next attack.

**Ending the scene.** When nothing hostile is awake, the scene ends. Stable heroes wake with 1 point. Spells and relics that last “this scene” end, and any once-a-scene item may be used again.

## 7. Hurt, dying and healing (new)

At **0 points** you fall and are **dying**. **Roll a d4: that is your countdown, in rounds.** Write it where everyone can see it.

- **Timing.** At the end of *every round*, after the foes have acted, **the countdown of each dying hero drops by 1**. At 0 the hero is dead.
- **Being hit again.** A blow that gets through on a dying hero takes **a round off the countdown** instead of rolling damage.
- **Tending.** An ally in reach uses an **Action** and makes a WIS check (Easy −3 with bandages). On a success the hero is **stable**: the countdown stops and they stay at 0 until healed or the scene ends. On a critical they also recover 1d4.
- **Healing.** Any healing of 1 or more stands a dying or stable hero up with that many points.
- **Stable and hurt.** A stable hero who is hurt again becomes dying again and rolls a new d4.
- **Dead.** The hero stays dead. *(Death Bargains from the existing game are set aside as a later optional module.)*

**Healing in play.** Each source of healing works **once per person per rest**, unless that person is dying or down:

| Source | Heals |
|---|---|
| Rest at a hearth | 1 Heart (10 points) |
| Linen bandages | d6 (WIS check) |
| Mend (the Litany) | d8 (WIS check, 1 Horror) |
| Saint’s knucklebone | d8 (no roll, 1 Horror) |

Nothing heals without limit; the hearth is where the party recovers.

## 8. The Looming Horror

The Horror is **one shared track, 0 to 21**, written where everyone can see it. It starts at 0. It has three thresholds: **9 The Stirring**, **15 The Turning** and **21 The Arrival** **(kept)**.

### What adds Horror (changed)

Horror rises only by **voluntary choices**. Failing a check does not add it, and taking damage does not add it **(changed: the existing game raised it on failure and on Wounds)**.

| Choice | Cost |
|---|---|
| A Minor spell or prayer (Ember, Mend, Ward, Kindle) | **+1** |
| A Greater working (Unmake, Speak with the Dead) | **+2** |
| An Ultimate working (Rend the Veil) | **+3** |
| A relic | as the relic says, **+1 to +3** |
| A push | **+1** |

**When it is paid.** The cost is paid **at the moment you roll for the thing**, or, for something with no roll, the moment you declare it, **and it is paid whether the roll succeeds or fails**. If you are *refused* before the roll (the effect is not allowed, the item is used up, the Arrival has stopped all magic) nothing is paid. A push is paid when you take the second Action, so it cannot be paid and then lost. The push cost and the Action’s own cost are separate and both paid; nothing is paid twice for the same thing.

### The thresholds (kept, tidied)

A threshold is reached when the track reaches or passes it. When it is:

1. The GM says aloud *“The ladder reaches 9: the Stirring.”*
2. **At the end of the Action that crossed it**, before the next turn, the GM rolls the **aftershock** d6 for that threshold and reads it out. The tables are in §10.
3. A threshold **fires once**. It fires again only after the track has fallen **below** it and then climbed back.

### Edge cases (new)

- **Crossing several thresholds at once.** Resolve each in ascending order, 9 then 15 then 21, one aftershock roll each, after the action that crossed them.
- **Falling below and crossing again.** Resting from 11 to 7 re-arms the 9. Rising to 9 later fires it again. Staying at or above 9 does not.
- **At 21.** The track does not rise further. **Each point that would** is paid instead by whoever caused it as **Strain: 2 damage that nothing reduces.** A Minor spell at 21 costs its caster 2; an Ultimate working, 6. Magic and pushing are therefore never free, and a hero may decline. Strain can drop a hero to 0.
- **The Arrival forbids magic** (aftershock result 3). While it holds, no spell is cast and no relic used; nothing is attempted, so nothing is paid.
- **Healing magic outside combat.** It costs Horror exactly as in combat, and works once per person per rest; the second casting on the same person heals nothing and still costs.
- **Several heroes at once.** Every cost is applied to the one track in the order it happens at the table (clockwise within a round). Two costs paid in the same action are added together first. Thresholds are resolved after the whole action.

### Resting (changed)

Only a **party rest in a safe place** lowers the track.

- **A safe place (a hearth).** The GM marks it when planning, and says so when the party arrives. It is a place the party can **hold for a watch**: a door that bars, hallowed ground, a room behind a locked reliquary door, with **nothing hostile awake that knows they are there**. Open ground is not safe. A hearth is used **once**; resting twice at the same hearth does nothing. Expect one hearth for each stage of an adventure.
- **The watch.** A rest takes one whole watch of the night. *If it is interrupted* (a foe arrives, an aftershock decides it) **nothing is restored, and the hearth is still there**.
- **What it does.** Every living hero recovers **1 Heart** and refreshes the once-per-rest healing and once-per-scene items. The track falls by **a d6 for every two living heroes** (one or two: 1d6; three or four: 2d6; five or six: 3d6). It never goes below 0.
- **When it does not.** The Turning’s “troubled night” (result 3) removes the Horror reduction once, but not the healing. The Arrival’s “dawn does not come” (result 6) forbids resting in that place at all.

## 9. Magic, relics and grimoires (new)

Magic is **in objects**. A **grimoire** holds three spells (1 or 2 slots); a **relic** does one dangerous thing (1 or 2 slots). Neither is learned. You can cast from a book you carry, and nobody else needs the knowledge. Lose the book and you lose the spells; give it to a friend and they can read it, at their own risk. A new spell is a new item.

## 10. Aftershock tables

Roll a d6 at the end of the Action that crossed the threshold. Rows marked *kept* are the existing tables word for word; *adapted* rows changed to fit these rules (the old text is in brackets).

**9. The Stirring.** *Something small and wrong.*

1. Cold breath: every hero’s next Move this round is lost. *(adapted: was “no reaction next round”)*
2. A whisper names one hero. Their next check is Hard (+3). *(adapted: was −2)*
3. The animals panic. The next check to move quietly or to calm anything is Hard (+3). *(kept)*
4. The dead stir: add one weak foe, such as a plague rat swarm, to the scene. *(kept)*
5. Shadows lag: every hurt hero takes 2 damage that nothing reduces. *(adapted: was “1 more harm”)*
6. A false calm. Nothing happens, but the next aftershock roll is at +1 (a 7 counts as a 6). *(kept)*

**15. The Turning.** *The land answers.*

1. The ground shakes: every hero takes 4 damage that nothing reduces. *(adapted: was 2 harm)*
2. The foes swell: each rolls its Effort one size larger until the scene ends (to a maximum of d12). *(adapted: was an extra damage die)*
3. The night is troubled: the next rest does not lower the Looming Horror. *(kept)*
4. The way out closes. Leaving takes a Hard (+3) check, and each failure adds 1 Horror. *(kept)*
5. Reinforcements: the GM adds a foe of their choice. *(kept)*
6. The Horror feeds: every spell and relic adds 1 more Horror until the track falls below 15. *(kept)*

**21. The Arrival.** *Whatever waited has come.*

1. The thing at the bottom of the ladder stands in the room: bring on the scene’s greatest foe. *(kept)*
2. Every hero takes 5 damage (half a Heart) that nothing reduces. *(adapted: was a Wound, 4 harm)*
3. No magic can be cast and no relic used until the track falls below 21. Nothing is attempted, so nothing is paid. *(adapted: was “all magic fails”)*
4. The floor opens: each hero passes a Hard (+3) DEX check or falls somewhere apart from the rest. *(kept)*
5. One hero is taken. The others find them with a Hard (+3) WIS check, or lose them. *(adapted: was Heroic 18)*
6. Dawn does not come: the party cannot rest in this place. *(kept)*

## 11. For the GM

**Sizing a fight.** Add up the foes’ Hearts and compare with the number of heroes.

| Fight | Foe Hearts per hero | What it costs the party |
|---|---|---|
| Skirmish | ½ | Almost nothing. |
| Standard | 1 | About a quarter of the party’s health; sometimes someone falls. |
| Hard | 1½ | About half; someone usually falls. Dying earns its place. |
| Deadly | 1¾ or more | Leave room to retreat. |

**Timers.** To keep the pressure on, set a timer: a named count (“the bell tolls”, 4). It falls by one at the end of every round, or when the GM takes one off by hand. At 0, the thing happens; a timer can be set to start again at 0, for something that keeps coming. Players see every timer; only the GM changes them.

These come from a crude simulation (see `validation.md`), so treat them as a starting point to correct in play.

**Setting the TN.** Start every scene at Wary or Dangerous. Raise it for a place or a foe that is plainly better than the party. Leave it alone when the party is clever; reward cleverness with an Easy.

**Aiming the Horror.** Do not add Horror yourself. Offer choices that tempt: a locked door that magic opens in a moment, a wounded friend, a foe that will not be stopped by iron. The Horror is the price players choose to pay for convenience. Your job is to make sure the convenient thing is tempting and the consequence is real.

**Death Bargains.** The existing Death Bargain table is set aside. It may return as an optional module for tables that want a way back from death at a cost.
