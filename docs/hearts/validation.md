# Hearts prototype: validation

Generated by `node scripts/hearts/validate.mjs`. **Read this first:** the first five sections are arithmetic on the rules and are exact. The last two use a crude simulation (a fixed policy that never positions, talks or improvises, with everyone within reach of everyone). They show whether the numbers hang together. They are **not evidence that the game is balanced or fun**; only a human playtest can say that.

## 1. Chance of success on a d20 check

A natural 1 always fails and a natural 20 always succeeds, so no check is better than 95% or worse than 5%. The modifier is a score plus gear, never more than +5.

| Modifier | TN 10 | TN 12 | TN 14 | TN 16 | Easy at 12 (9) | Hard at 12 (15) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| +0 | 55% | 45% | 35% | 25% | 60% | 30% |
| +1 | 60% | 50% | 40% | 30% | 65% | 35% |
| +2 | 65% | 55% | 45% | 35% | 70% | 40% |
| +3 | 70% | 60% | 50% | 40% | 75% | 45% |
| +4 | 75% | 65% | 55% | 45% | 80% | 50% |
| +5 | 80% | 70% | 60% | 50% | 85% | 55% |

A starting hero has a best score of +2: a 55% chance against the usual fight Target Number of 12, 60% against a wary scene. Gear adds to a score at most +2 in total (armour only to defence), so nobody reaches the cap before the equipment is excellent. Nothing is automatic.

## 2. What an Action is worth

Expected damage from one attack at a modifier of +2 against TN 12 (success 55%, 5% of those criticals that add a d12). Expected damage = average of the die × (chance of success + 5%).

| Source | Effort | Horror | Damage per Action | Against an armoured foe | Damage per Horror point |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fists or an improvised weapon (Basic, +1) | d4 + 1 | 0 | 2.3 | 2.3 | — |
| Cudgel, hatchet, spear or bill hook (Weapons) | d6 + 3 | 0 | 4.2 | 3.6 | — |
| Hunting bow or windlass crossbow (Ranged; the crossbow fires every other Action unless pushed) | d8 + 3 | 0 | 4.9 | 4.9 | — |
| Ember (Magic) | d10 + 3 | 1 | 5.5 | 5.5 | 5.5 |
| Unmake (Magic, also Dazes the foe) | d10 + 3 | 2 | 5.5 | 5.5 | 2.8 |
| Executioner’s blade (relic, Magic) | d10 + 3 | 1 | 5.5 | 5.5 | 5.5 |
| Rend the Veil, one target (Ultimate, once a scene) | d12 + 0 | 3 | 4.2 | 4.2 | 1.4 |

Weapons, ranged weapons and magic roll d6, d8 and d10 (the Effort categories), so magic is the biggest die, and it is paid for in Horror. A hero who casts every turn adds roughly 1 Horror a round. A standard fight lasts 3 to 5 rounds in the simulation (section 6), so a party of casters reaches the first threshold (9) in a fight or two and the second (15) in two or three. That is the intended pace, and the reason the shared track matters. The ready-made heroes put three of their four Effort points into their main kind of blow, so the table above assumes +3 (a hero with 0 in a kind rolls the bare die).
Rend the Veil is once a scene: against four foes in Close it deals about 16.9 in total for 3 Horror, which is the best rate in the book, and the reason a Doomed scene should be hard to reach.

## 3. Action economy and pushing

A turn is one Move and one Action. A push gives a second Action once a turn for 1 Horror (and costs its own cost on top: a pushed spell is 2 Horror, a pushed Strike is 1).

| A hero’s turn | Horror | Expected damage |
|---|---|---|
| Strike with a d6 weapon (+3) | 0 | 4.2 |
| Strike, then push and Strike again | 1 | 8.5 |
| Cast Ember (d10 + 3) | 1 | 5.5 |
| Cast Ember, push, cast Ember | 3 | 11.1 |
| Cast Unmake, push, cast Unmake | 5 | 11.1 |

Pushing a weapon Strike buys one extra Strike for 1 Horror: the cheapest damage in the game per point of Horror. That is why pushing is limited to once a turn and why the cost cannot be waived at 21 (section 5).

## 4. Healing and recovery

A hero starts with 1 Heart (10 points). Each source heals a given person once per rest, so there is no loop that restores the party from inside a fight without limit.

| Source | Heals | Roll | Limit | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rest at a hearth | 10 (1 Heart) | none | once per hearth | one watch; takes a d6 off the Horror for every two heroes (1 to 2 heroes: 1d6, 3 to 4: 2d6, 5 to 6: 3d6) |
| Linen bandages | d6 (average 3.5) | WIS check | 3 uses, once per person per rest | none |
| Mend (spell) | d8 (average 4.5) | WIS check | once per person per rest | 1 Horror each, paid even on a miss |
| Saint’s knucklebone | d8 (average 4.5) | none | once per person per rest | 1 Horror |
| Aqua vitae | d6 | none | 1 use | none |

The most one person can be healed between rests, from every source, is about 13.0 on average (23.0 with the rest itself), against 10 points of health. Healing is therefore real but cannot undo a bad fight for free: to heal four people with Mend costs at least 4 Horror, and a second casting on the same person heals nothing and still costs 1.

**Repeated casting outside combat.** Horror is paid whether or not anyone is fighting, and only a rest in a safe place takes it down. A healer who wants the party at full health before the next room spends Horror to do it; there is no free refill.

## 5. Inventory

Ten slots. Armour and heavy weapons take more than one. Relics and grimoires take 1 or 2.

A hero in plate harness, with a bill hook and a shield, uses all ten slots: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6, leaving four for everything else. Carrying a grimoire and a relic as well makes a character who can do a lot, and who is the most dangerous person in the party to the Looming Horror.

Reading the table: heroes start with one Heart, but six points of stats and four of Effort let them hit hard. A **skirmish** (×0.5 Hearts per hero) is nearly free, a **standard** fight (×1) costs about a quarter of the party’s health and sometimes puts someone on the floor, a **hard** fight (×1.5) costs about half and kills someone about one time in three, and anything near ×1.75 is a fight the party should be able to leave. Smaller parties are hit harder per head because the simulated healers and tenders have fewer hands.

## 7. Horror pacing (simulated)

3000 simulated evenings per row. An evening is the shape of the sample adventure: a standard fight, a rest at a hearth, a hard fight, a second rest at a hearth, and a boss fight (the Hollow Bishop with escorts). Casters spend up to the budget shown; pushing happens on the given share of turns. The track starts at 0.

| Party | Casters’ budget | Pushes | Median peak | Reach 9 | Reach 15 | Reach 21 | Strain taken | Heroes who die | Evening survived |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 6 | 0% | 6 | 0% | 0% | 0% | 0.0 | 0.9 | 83% |
| 2 | 12 | 10% | 12 | 85% | 2% | 0% | 0.0 | 0.8 | 85% |
| 2 | 21 | 30% | 21 | 94% | 78% | 51% | 1.3 | 0.7 | 86% |
| 3 | 6 | 0% | 7 | 18% | 0% | 0% | 0.0 | 1.3 | 88% |
| 3 | 12 | 10% | 13 | 93% | 31% | 1% | 0.0 | 1.2 | 91% |
| 3 | 21 | 30% | 21 | 98% | 90% | 73% | 5.7 | 1.0 | 93% |
| 4 | 6 | 0% | 7 | 22% | 0% | 0% | 0.0 | 1.3 | 96% |
| 4 | 12 | 10% | 14 | 96% | 45% | 2% | 0.0 | 1.2 | 97% |
| 4 | 21 | 30% | 21 | 99% | 93% | 84% | 9.4 | 0.9 | 99% |
| 5 | 6 | 0% | 7 | 21% | 0% | 0% | 0.0 | 1.6 | 99% |
| 5 | 12 | 10% | 14 | 96% | 41% | 3% | 0.1 | 1.2 | 99% |
| 5 | 21 | 30% | 21 | 100% | 95% | 80% | 9.5 | 0.9 | 99% |

Reading the table: a *restrained* party (casters stop at a budget of 6, nobody pushes) mostly stays at or under the first threshold. A *measured* party (budget 12, a tenth of turns pushed) usually reaches 15 and meets the Arrival in about one evening in five. A *reckless* party (no budget, three turns in ten pushed) reaches the Arrival every time and then pays Strain for everything it does afterwards. Because rests lower the track by a d6 for every two heroes, a bigger party recovers more per rest to match the extra hands casting and pushing: without that scaling the same measured play reaches 21 in about half the evenings at four or five heroes (an earlier run of this model). The numbers are for a particular made-up policy and one adventure shape; read them as a starting point for the first real session, not a verdict.

## What this does not show

- Whether fights are fun or tense. Rounds and health lost are blunt proxies.
- Positioning. The simulation puts everyone in reach of everyone, so Reach, Hook, Dazed and the distance bands matter more than it shows.
- Table talk, clever use of the fiction, or any plan that is not "attack the weakest foe".
- Exploration, obstacles and the story around the fights. The adventure tests those; this cannot.
- Balance of the relics and grimoires in anything but their damage and cost.
