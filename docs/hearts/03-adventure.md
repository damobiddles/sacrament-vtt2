# The Drowned Reliquary of Saint Odrin

*A one-session adventure for three to five heroes (the four ready heroes will do). About three hours. Built to test the Hearts rules: a Hearts obstacle, a fight in each of three sizes, a choice about magic, pushing under a clock, two hearths and the Looming Horror from 0 to the Arrival.*

**The premise.** The plague year has emptied the chapter house at Odrin’s Rest. In the night the drowned bell of the saint’s chapel began to ring under the water, and the dead in the village churchyard have started walking towards the sound. The Hollow Bishop, who drowned himself rather than bury another child, has taken the bell. The heroes are sent to silence it. If they cannot break the bell, they must at least take the saint’s reliquary out of its reach.

**Before play.** Hand out the four ready heroes. Put the Horror track (0 to 21) and the scene TN where everybody can see them. Say the rule aloud: *“Spells, relics and pushing add Horror. Nothing else does. Rest in a hearth takes it down.”* Start the Horror at 0.

---

## 1. The Causeway (Wary, TN 10)

*The flooded approach. The tide is coming in.*

The stone causeway to the chapel is half under black water. The only way in is through the sluice-house, where the old winch raises the gate and drains the lower crypt. The winch is jammed with a drowned man’s coat.

**Obstacle: the jammed winch, 2 Hearts.** Anyone may work at it with a STR check. Each success rolls an Effort die and adds their Effort points (d4 bare hands, d6 with a tool, the hatchet or the bill hook as a lever, **+d6 more if the grapnel is used to pull**) and adds it to the progress (20 to finish). Another hero may Assist.

**The tide is a clock.** Set a **timer** on the live table when the work begins: *The water rises*, **3**, falling each round and not repeating. (On paper, tick a die down from 3.) When it reaches 0 the lower crypt is flooded and **every check made in the crypt is Hard (+3)** until a rest. Hauling the winch clear before then stops the tide: the GM removes the timer.

**Failure costs:** the winch screams (the GM may add a plague rat swarm to the next fight) or the working hero takes 1d4 that nothing reduces.

*What this tests:* the Effort die on a non-combat task, Assist, a clock, and the first temptation to push for a second Action. A *Kindle* or *Ember* to cut the coat away (**+1 Horror**) is a legitimate shortcut.

## 2. The Ossuary (Dangerous, TN 12), standard fight

*Skulls in niches. Water to the ankle. Something is wading towards the light.*

Foes for four heroes (about 4 Hearts, a Heart a hero): **two gaunt penitents** and **two plague rat swarms**. For three heroes, one penitent and two swarms; for five, add a grave hound.

The penitents come up the stairs; the swarms are in the niches and come when the first blow lands. The Black Candle (if anyone has it) shows both before they arrive.

*What this tests:* a standard fight (expect 4 to 6 rounds), defence checks, the bill hook, Reach, the shield, a hero going down and the d4 countdown. A caster who casts every round will cross **9, the Stirring**, here. Roll the aftershock at the end of the Action that did it.

## 3. The Sacristy (a hearth)

*A dry vestry behind an iron-bound door that bars from within.* The party may rest here. If the GM says the rest is safe and the party takes a whole watch, they recover as in the rules. **Interrupt it only once,** if you like, with a foe at the door and an aftershock: nothing is restored, and the party may try again.

*What this tests:* resting, the Horror reduction (a d6 per two heroes), the once-per-rest healing, and that an interrupted rest costs nothing.

## 4. The Reliquary Door (Dangerous, TN 12), a choice

*An oak door banded in silver, with the saint’s face worn off the lock.* Behind it is the way to the font and the altar. There are four ways through:

- **Picks.** An obstacle, **3 Hearts**, with Locksmith’s picks (DEX; Effort d6 for the picks and d6 more if the lantern is lit). Quiet, slow, no Horror. Each failure adds a tick to the next fight (see below).
- **The password.** *Speak with the Dead* on the porter’s corpse in the niche beside it (WIS; **2 Horror**). It answers: *“Ask the bell.”*
- **Unmake.** Smash the lock apart (INT; **2 Horror**). Loud: the foes at the font start the next fight awake and **Engaged** with the first hero through.
- **The bill hook, with muscle.** A Hard (+3) STR check, an Effort d6 (plus Effort points) against 2 Hearts of door. Fast, honest, and loud.

*What this tests:* a real choice between time, noise and Horror. The party reaching 15, the Turning, here is a good sign.

## 5. The Font (Dangerous, TN 12 or Deadly 14), hard fight

*A flooded baptistery. The font is a black ring of water; things stand in it.*

Foes for four heroes (about 6 Hearts, a Heart and a half a hero): **a drowned sexton** (2 Hearts, armoured, d10) and **two grave hounds** (1 Heart each, fast, d8), with **two gaunt penitents**. Make it TN 14 if the party arrives at 15 Horror or more, and say so: at one Heart each, someone may well fall.

The sexton is armoured: plain weapons under d8 step down. A hero with the crossbow, a relic blade or a spell shrugs this off; a bill hook or a cudgel is slowed to a d4. Holy oil makes it worse for the sexton (unholy): Effort steps up one size.

*What this tests:* a hard fight, armoured foes, the dying countdown under pressure, healing under a Horror cost, and a party deciding whether to retreat to the hearth.

## 6. The Bell Loft (an optional hearth)

*A ladder in the baptistery leads up to a dry loft under the bell tower.* Reaching it takes **an obstacle: 1 Heart, STR, with the rope** (without it, Hard). It is a hearth, and a bad one: **the bell is directly above**. Resting here takes the track down as usual, but the GM rolls a d6 at the start of the watch; **on a 1 or 2** the bell rings, the rest is interrupted, and **the Hollow Bishop is awake** for the next scene.

*What this tests:* a rest with a price, and the party choosing between a risk and a worse fight.

## 7. The Altar of the Drowned Bell (Deadly, TN 14), the boss

*A drowned altar, a hanging bell as tall as a man, and a bishop in sodden vestments.*

**The Hollow Bishop:** 5 Hearts, Effort d12, one attack a turn, defended with **WIS**; **when his attack gets through, that hero adds 1 Horror**. He is accompanied by **two gaunt penitents** (about 7 Hearts for four heroes, with the bell’s swarms to come; for five add an escort, and for three begin the Bishop at 4 Hearts).

**The bell.** Set a **timer** on the live table before the fight: *The bell tolls*, **3**, falling each round and **starting again at 0**. Each time it reaches 0 the bell rings and the GM adds a **plague rat swarm** to the room. (On paper, tick a die down from 3 and reset it.) The heroes can stop it, and when they do the GM removes the timer: **breaking the bell is a 2 Hearts obstacle** worked as an Action (STR; Effort d6 with the bill hook or the hatchet; a relic blade rolls d10). Every Action spent on the bell is an Action not spent on the Bishop.

**The Arrival waits.** If the track reaches 21, roll the Arrival aftershock; if it is already 21, every spell and push is paid in Strain, and the party must decide how much they are willing to pay.

*What this tests:* a boss and an obstacle in one scene, shared Horror at its peak, and the choice to push for the second Action.

## After the bell

The ringing stops when the bell is broken or the Bishop is down and the clapper is taken. Each hero chooses **one Discovery**:

- **The bell’s clapper** (a relic, 2 slots): Action, WIS check, **+2 Horror**; all foes in Close are Dazed *and* the Horror does not rise from the next push this scene.
- **Saint Odrin’s reliquary** (a relic, 1 slot): Action, no roll, **+1 Horror**; the whole party heals Effort d8 each once per rest.
- **A Mark**: +1 to one score (to a base of +3 at most).
- **A new item** of the GM’s choosing from the library.

## What to watch for at the table

1. **Do the thresholds happen at the right times?** The Stirring in the Ossuary, the Turning at the font, the Arrival only for a reckless party.
2. **Are the Horror costs a real choice?** Count how many times a player says “I’ll just…” and then does not.
3. **How long are the fights?** Count rounds. Anything over eight is too long.
4. **Is anyone bored?** Note who has nothing to do on their turn.
5. **Do the hearths work as a decision?** Does the party argue about whether to rest?
6. **Is dying frightening and fair?** Note the d4 rolls and the rescues.
7. **Did the equipment change what the heroes did?** Not just how hard they hit.

Write down the Horror track after every scene. It is the story of the night.
