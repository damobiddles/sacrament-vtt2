Sacrament was first played on the Sacrament RPG Table, in a browser. The rules work just as well on paper. You need one GM, three to five players, a set of dice each, and a few printed sheets. No screens, no accounts.

{{PLAY_PATHS}}

## What to bring

- **One GM and three to five players.** The GM describes the world and plays everyone in it; each player plays one hero.
- **Dice for each player:** a d20 for checks, plus a d4, d6, d8, d10 and d12 for Effort.
- **Dice for the GM:** two d10s for d100 loot rolls, and a d6 for a Party rest.
- **A hero sheet for each hero,** and pencils with erasers. HP, charges and Horror go up and down all evening.
- **The print-and-play kit** (below): the quick reference, the Horror track, the Target sign, foe cards, turn slips, countdowns and a healing log.
- **A coin** to mark the Horror, and another to mark the round.
- **Three candles** (optional, and worth it). Light them before the first scene, and keep a lamp to read by. Snuff one when the Horror reaches 7, the second at 14 and the last at 21. The room gets darker as the Horror gets closer. Never leave a flame alone.
- **A map and pieces.** A printed map or a wipe-clean mat, and miniatures, tokens or coins. No grid is needed: distances are Close, Near, Far and Very Far.
- **A GM screen** or a folder, to hide private rolls, notes and the adventure.
- **About two hours** for The Rib of Saint Hussey, or **about four** for The Guest at Greave, in one long session or two shorter ones.

## The adventure: The Guest at Greave

A ready-to-run adventure in five scenes, written for a real table. The heroes carry the winter supplies up to a snowbound priory whose signal fire has gone out, and find eight people they have never met and a question nobody wants to ask out loud. It is for three to five starting heroes and takes about four hours. The fights are short; the real game is who you would trust to hold the lamp.

**The adventure and its audio are for the GM only:** they tell you who the monster is. Players get the opening reading and the map.

## A shorter adventure: The Rib of Saint Hussey

One evening, about two hours. The heroes carry a saint's rib out of the city of Aubrecht to his chapel at Wendholt by dusk on his feast day, with a thief, the city watch, a canon's hired riders and the drowned dead in the way. It is for three to five starting heroes, and it is all chases and short fights.

**The GM pack is for the GM only:** the adventure, the plot on one page and the GM cards give away every secret. The pack also has a blank hero sheet and the rules cheat sheet, so one download is everything you need to print.

## Before the game

**Players**

1. **Make a hero.** Open [My heroes](/character/), press **Generate a hero**, then **Print or save as PDF** to get a filled-in character sheet. Or fill in the blank hero sheet below by hand. Both have the Marks box for growing stronger.
2. **Print the rules cheat sheet**, or read it once on your phone. It covers checks, Effort, Push, the Horror, healing and dying, and what story spells do.
3. **Do not read the adventure.** If your GM runs The Guest at Greave, the only part meant for players is the opening reading, Before the Snow. The Rib of Saint Hussey has no player handouts: the GM reads the opening aloud.

**The GM**

1. **Print the print-and-play kit.** Sheet 9 is a checklist of everything else to bring.
2. **Read the adventure** all the way through, and listen to the narrated version if it helps.
3. **Prepare the foe and obstacle cards** for the first scene, and keep them face down until the heroes find them.
4. **Print the [Current Loot Tables](/loot/)** page, or keep it open on a phone. You roll on it when the heroes search.
5. **Decide the house rules** and tell the players before you start: whether a natural 20 adds the Ultimate d12, and whether scene danger adds to monster attacks. Both are optional.

## The rules at a glance

- **Checks:** roll d20 and add your score. Meet or beat the Target to succeed. Easy lowers the Target by 3; Hard raises it by 3.
- **Effort:** after a success, when the amount matters, roll the Effort die for what you used and add your Effort bonus. Ordinary weapons, bows and crossbows use d6; guns use d8 (and misfire on a natural 1 to 3); damaging magic uses d10. Every two-handed weapon adds +2 damage. A successful natural 20 adds a plain d12.
- **Defence:** 10 + CON + armour. A monster rolls d20 + its attack bonus against it. A monster's natural 1 always misses and its natural 20 always hits.
- **Hearts:** 10 HP to a Heart. At 0 HP a hero is dying for 3 rounds. On their turn they may crawl away (a free CON check) or pay 1 Horror to act anyway.
- **Push:** after any failed roll, once, on your own turn. It costs 1 Horror and gives you an immediate second action, which cannot be pushed.
- **Paths:** once a scene, a check that fits your path is Easy, if the GM agrees. Never an attack.
- **The Horror:** one shared track from 0 to 21. Every spell except healing adds 1. Items add their listed cost. A Push adds 1. A hero's natural 1 adds 1 more only on magic or a pushed action. At 7, 14 and 21 the Horror strikes, once an adventure each: 7, every hero's next roll is Hard; 14, every foe met from then on has 2 more HP; 21, no more rest. Past 21 you pay in blood: each point that cannot go on the track costs the hero who caused it 1 HP.
- **Party rest:** when the GM allows it, each living hero heals 10 HP and the Horror falls by one d6.

The [Player guide](/player-guide/) and [GM guide](/gm-guide/) have the full rules. They describe the buttons of the online table, but the numbers are the same on paper. The rules are still being playtested: check [Rules updates and errata](/rules/updates/) before you print, and correct older printouts by hand.
