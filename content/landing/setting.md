## The world
Sacrament is set in a dark age of stone churches, plague carts and printing presses, ruled by humans. Other peoples are feared and despised. The Church teaches that they sided with the demons in the Age of Ash, or were made by them. Most people have never met one, and most are glad of it.

## Three ages
**The Age of Ash.** Demons ruled and men were slaves. The sky was the colour of a cold hearth.

**The Binding.** The First Saints sealed the demons beneath the earth, at terrible cost. Few of them lived to see it hold. Their names are feast days now.

**The Hallowed Age.** This one. The Church keeps the Binding, and everyone who lives above it.

## The Church
It keeps the calendar, the schools and most of the hospitals. It has a pontiff and cardinals, monastic orders, relics set in silver, pilgrim roads and indulgences for sale.

The Inquisition hunts witches and unsanctioned magic. Its officers are lawyers and confessors first. A summons from them is rarely the end of the story, and never a good beginning.

The Order of the Vault keeps captured monsters in the Undervaults beneath Aubrecht, the capital, to study them. Little of what the brothers learn is published.

## Magic and the Horror
Magic is forbidden. The Church says that using it tempts the Horror, which takes many forms: a hunger, a stranger in a familiar face, a quiet that follows a person home. Rites the Church sanctions are another matter. The difference is ordination.

## The land
Nobles hold the heartlands, inside the consecrated boundaries: wayshrines at the crossroads, boundary stones along the fields. There are roads, tithes and market days. Grimsby Fold, Hollowmere and Saint Aubrel's Cross are places like these.

Beyond lie the Unhallowed lands: wild, unexplored, full of ruins from before the Binding. Few go. Fewer come back with the same number of friends.

## Monsters
They are hunted, or taken alive and kept below the city. Common folk name them for what they have been seen to do: the Pit Ghoul, the Drowned Acolyte, the Stair Wraith.
